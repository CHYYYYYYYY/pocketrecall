# LEDGER / HD-EPIC kitchen research replay

This non-commercial hackathon demonstration uses the real egocentric kitchen video and public research exports from [LEDGER — Never Look Back: Understanding Persistence in 3D Object Memory from Egocentric Videos](https://ledger-3d.github.io/) by Chaudhari and colleagues. The underlying video belongs to [HD-EPIC](https://hd-epic.github.io/site/), distributed under [Creative Commons Attribution–NonCommercial 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc/4.0/). Credit and non-commercial restrictions apply to these dataset assets; the application code does not change their license. No endorsement is implied.

Official inputs:

- [Unmodified video](https://ledger-3d.github.io/assets/hdepic/video.mp4), saved as `kitchen.mp4`.
- [Object histories and camera samples](https://ledger-3d.github.io/assets/hdepic/data.json).
- [Pi3X + TSDF cloud metadata](https://ledger-3d.github.io/assets/hdepic/dense.json) and [binary cloud](https://ledger-3d.github.io/assets/hdepic/dense.bin).
- [LEDGER research code](https://github.com/LEDGER-3D/LEDGER) and [public demo source](https://github.com/LEDGER-3D/ledger-3d.github.io).

`scripts/prepare-ledger.py` creates 41 resized JPEG captures at four-second intervals, six selected object histories and downsampled point-cloud increments. It rotates the source coordinate system to Y-up. A 100-degree approximate frustum and coarse 64×64 depth buffer reveal reference points along the exported camera trajectory. Original point acquisition times, full camera calibration and roll are unavailable: the reveal is illustrative, not newly computed reconstruction or proof of when a point was first seen. Camera roll is approximated as zero; coordinates are conservatively labeled relative, without verified metric scale.

Object ID 39 is the wine glass. Its exported sightings are at **16, 36, 40, 92, 96, 128, 136 and 144 seconds**. These are LEDGER model outputs, not human-verified ground truth. No sampled detection between 40 and 92 seconds establishes only a gap in those outputs. It does not prove physical absence, identity correctness or a successful lost-object search. PocketRecall saves each sample when playback reaches it; future sighting records are not inserted into session memory.

`user-0.wav` through `user-3.wav` are generated English questions created for this demonstration with the system speech synthesizer, converted to mono 16 kHz PCM. They contain no recorded user microphone input. Responses are generated by the configured Gemini Live service during execution, or explicitly labeled browser speech fallback when no key is configured.
